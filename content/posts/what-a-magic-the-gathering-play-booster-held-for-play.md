From Murders at Karlov Manor (February 9, 2024) through Reality Fracture (October 2, 2026), the pack you drafted, played sealed with, and opened at a prerelease was the Play Booster. Here is what it held as game pieces: how many cards, at what rarity, and what that meant for building a deck.

## The pack

A Play Booster held 14 cards: 13 from the set, The List, or Special Guests, plus a land. Wizards' 2023 template:

| Slot         | Cards | Rarity                          |
| ------------ | ----- | ------------------------------- |
| Commons      | 6     | Common                          |
| Seventh card | 1     | Common, or a card from The List |
| Uncommons    | 3     | Uncommon                        |
| Wildcard     | 1     | Any rarity                      |
| Rare         | 1     | Rare or mythic rare             |
| Foil slot    | 1     | Any rarity                      |
| Land         | 1     | Basic land                      |

A token, ad, or art card came with it; it wasn't a card you could put in a deck.

Each set bent the template. The commons, the rare, the foil slot, and the land stayed; the rest moved with the set's design.

## Reality Fracture, the last Play Booster

Reality Fracture published odds for every slot. Counting every version of a card at its rarity:

| Slot               | Cards | Rarity                                                                                |
| ------------------ | ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Commons            | 5–6   | Common; in 1 of 55 packs, a Special Guests card replaces one                          |
| Uncommon           | 1     | Uncommon                                                                              |
| Common or uncommon | 1     | Uncommon about 77%, common 23%                                                        |
| Echoed Pairs       | 3     | Uncommon about 90%, rare about 7%, mythic about 2%                                    |
| Rare               | 1     | Rare about 83%, mythic about 16%                                                      |
| Foil slot          | 1     | Common 49.5%, uncommon 40.5%, rare 6%, mythic 1.2%; special versions make up the rest |
| Land               | 1     | Common dual land 54.5%, basic 45.5%                                                   |

## What every pack guaranteed

- At least one rare. About one rare slot in six was a mythic. The Echoed Pairs and the foil slot could add more, but most packs held one.
- Mostly commons and uncommons. They made up most of every Limited deck, and in Reality Fracture uncommons outnumbered the usual three, at about five a pack.
- A land that sometimes mattered. A basic added nothing, since basics are free at every event. A common dual land, in a little over half of Reality Fracture's packs, was a real pick for a two-color deck.

## The List and Special Guests

The List and Special Guests were cards from outside the set, reprinted into its packs. The first Play Boosters put a List card in the seventh slot about one pack in eight; by Reality Fracture, only Special Guests remained, in 1 pack in 55. They were legal in that set's draft and sealed decks like any card you opened, but they weren't designed for its Limited environment, so you judged each by what it did in your deck, not by its rarity.

## What a draft or sealed pool held

- Draft. Each player opened three packs, so a pod of eight opened 24 packs and at least 24 rares, three per player. You made 42 picks and played 23 or so of them.
- Sealed. A prerelease gave each player six Play Boosters, 84 cards with at least six rares, plus one extra rare or mythic. You built a 40-card deck from that pool.

---
title: Elections alone don't give the people lasting power
description: A vote is a conditional claim on power. Who picks the candidates and what offices can actually do decide what it is worth. What would give ordinary people power that lasts between elections?
date: 2026-11-26
---

Power has never lived in the ballot. It lives in the capacity to make decisions take effect, or to stop them. Ownership, organization, money, administrative authority, and control over force all supply that capacity.

A vote is a conditional claim on some of that power. What it is worth depends on who chooses the candidates, what offices can actually do, whether officials carry out decisions, and which matters lie beyond the reach of elections. Equal votes can coexist with deeply unequal command over those conditions.

But to say power never sat with the people concedes too much. People have exercised power through unions, movements, associations, and sustained collective action, and they have forced concessions that powerful institutions would not otherwise have made. Those were exercises of popular power, even when the power was temporary, uneven, or later dismantled.

The weakness is in treating the people as if we were already an organized actor. We are millions of people with different interests, resources, and abilities to act together. An election briefly adds up our choices. It does not, by itself, give us lasting control over those who govern.

That points to a harder question than whether elections work: what would give ordinary people durable power that officials and owners must answer to between elections?

A republic can declare the people sovereign while leaving them poorly equipped to act as sovereigns. The declaration is no substitute for the equipment.

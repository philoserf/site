---
title: Unnoticed Final Moments
description: Lastness is assigned by memory, not perceived in the moment, so the injunction to notice which time is the last asks for something structurally unavailable.
date: 2026-09-11
---

The advice is meant kindly. There will be a last time you carry a child to bed, a last ordinary conversation with a friend, a last unremarkable Sunday in the house you grew up in—so pay attention, because no one will tell you which one it is. Notice more. Hold it while you have it.

It cannot be done, and not because we are careless. The thing we are told to notice is not there to be noticed.

Lastness is not a property of a moment. Nothing distinguishes the final conversation from the second-to-last one; no quality inheres in it that closer attention would have caught. What makes it the last is everything that failed to follow—a relation between that afternoon and a future that never arrived. The relation is real enough once it exists. It does not exist yet at the time we are being instructed to perceive it. Memory assigns lastness afterward, from a vantage that knows what came next, and that assignment is the first moment the property is there at all.

So the injunction asks attention to point at something not in the room. This is not a difficult perception. It is not a perception. You cannot attend harder to a fact that will not be a fact for another eleven years.

The damage is that the failure feels like ours anyway. The advice arrives as wisdom, cannot be followed, and then hands us the bill in retrospect. We didn't notice, so we must have been distracted, ungrateful, asleep at the moment that turned out to matter. It is a peculiarly cruel structure: it can be applied backward, only after the moment is unreachable, and only by a self who now knows something the earlier self had no access to. We are being convicted of failing to read a signal that was never broadcast.

What is true in the advice survives the correction, but it has to be stated differently. Attention does change experience, and it works on what is present. Finitude is present. That the sequence ends is a standing fact of every afternoon in it, available at all times, requiring no forecast and no identification. Which member of the sequence is terminal is available at none. The first can be attended to continuously and cheaply; the second cannot be attended to at all. Confusing them is what turns a livable awareness into an impossible vigilance, and then into guilt.

You did not miss it. There was nothing to miss—only an afternoon indistinguishable from the others, doing what afternoons do. Lastness is a verdict memory passes, not a signal the world sends, and no amount of noticing would have moved the trial forward.

This is where to start with Magic: The Gathering outside Commander, as of January 2027. It covers only Magic's own worlds and leaves out the crossover (Universes Beyond) sets.

## Where to play

A local game store is the center of paper Magic. Stores run Friday Night Magic, weekly drafts, and prereleases, and Wizards' store locator lists the ones near you.

To start at home, buy a Foundations product, either the Beginner Box or the Starter Collection. Foundations is Magic's core set for new players and stays legal in Standard until at least 2029, so nothing in it goes stale soon.

## Constructed

In Constructed, you build a 60-card deck ahead of time from cards you own, with at most four copies of any card except basic lands, plus an optional 15-card sideboard. Four formats are worth knowing:

- Standard uses only recent sets, which keeps it cheaper and always changing. It's the place to start.
- Pioneer uses every set from 2012 on; it's deeper and more expensive.
- Modern uses every set from 2003 on; it's faster and the most expensive of the four.
- Pauper allows only commons from any set; it's the cheapest paper format with real depth.

### Standard right now

These sets from Magic's own worlds are legal in Standard:

- Leaving January 29, 2027: Wilds of Eldraine, The Lost Caverns of Ixalan, Murders at Karlov Manor, Outlaws of Thunder Junction (with The Big Score), Bloomburrow, Duskmourn: House of Horror.
- Staying after the rotation: Foundations, Aetherdrift, Tarkir: Dragonstorm, Edge of Eternities, Lorwyn Eclipsed, Secrets of Strixhaven, Reality Fracture, and Nauctis: The Sunken Realm once it releases.

Standard didn't rotate in 2026. From 2027 on, it rotates with the first set of each year and keeps three years of sets, so the January 29 rotation removes the six 2023–2024 sets above. It lands on the first day of the Nauctis: The Sunken Realm prerelease; the set itself releases February 5.

The buying advice follows directly: spend on cards from the staying sets. A rare from the leaving six stops being legal in Standard on January 29.

### Picking a first deck

Choose one deck and learn it well rather than collecting a bit of everything. Aggressive decks, built on low-cost creatures in one or two colors, are the easiest to learn and the cheapest to build. Look up current Standard decklists on MTGGoldfish or MTGTop8, then pick one whose cards come mostly from the staying sets.

## Draft

In draft, you open a pack, keep one card, and pass the rest until every card is gone. After three packs you build a 40-card deck from what you took and play the other drafters. Sealed is the same idea without the passing: you open six packs and build from them. It's the format at prereleases.

The basic recipe for a draft deck:

- 40 cards: about 17 lands and 23 spells.
- Two colors. Stay open for the first few picks, then settle into the colors that keep coming to you.
- Around 15 creatures, spread across a curve with plenty of two- and three-drops (creatures that cost two or three mana).
- Take removal and bombs (cards that win the game on their own) early; take filler late.

Play in store drafts, the social version, which usually run the newest set.

### Which set to draft

Nauctis: The Sunken Realm is the next set from Magic's own worlds. Its prerelease runs January 29 to February 4, and it releases February 5, when store drafts move to it. Until then, store drafts run Star Trek, so draft Reality Fracture (released October 2, 2026) at home with friends.

## A first month

1. Learn the rules with the Foundations Beginner Box.
2. Draft Reality Fracture with friends.
3. Pick one Standard deck built from the staying sets and buy it as singles.
4. Sign up for the Nauctis prerelease at a local store; it opens January 29.

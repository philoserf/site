---
title: The meaning of doors
description: A door is where we make a wall change its mind. Most doors have no lock, yet a closed one stops nearly everyone. What holds it shut?
date: 2027-02-03
---

A door is where we make a wall change its mind.

We build walls because separation matters: inside from outside, shelter from weather, private from public, ours from everyone else's. But complete separation would make a shelter a prison. We still need to leave, admit people, carry things in, and see what made that noise.

So we leave a hole, then give ourselves the power to close it. The door lets us decide when the wall applies.

That gives an ordinary object a great deal of meaning. An open door can be an invitation; a closed one, privacy or rejection. A locked door protects someone and excludes someone else. A knock asks permission to cross a boundary only an inch thick.

Crossing a door changes what you may do. You enter a classroom, a courtroom, a bedroom. Same person, another set of permissions.

Most doors have no lock. A closed one stops nearly everyone anyway: the handle would turn, but we knock, or wait, or walk away. The lock is for the few who would ignore that. Everyone else stops at the wood.

The strange thing is how much of our world depends on an agreement about a hole: you could walk through, but first you must be let in.

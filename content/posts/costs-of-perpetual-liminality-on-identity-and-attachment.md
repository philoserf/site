---
title: Costs of perpetual liminality on identity and attachment
description: Perpetual transition builds ambiguity tolerance while leaving identity unconsolidated and attachments provisional or clingy. Chosen movement and movement as avoidance of vulnerability look identical from outside but differ from inside.
date: 2026-09-09
aliases:
  - Perpetual Transition and Identity
---

Perpetual liminality builds real capacities—tolerance for ambiguity, the trick of holding two views at once, comfort inside a question that won't resolve. It also builds a hole where identity was supposed to consolidate. Ask this person who they are and they'll tell you what they do, or where they've been. They get skilled at becoming. They never get to be.

Attachment takes the same shape. Connection needs return—proof it survives an absence—and perpetual transition never lets that proof accumulate. So the bonds stay provisional, or they tip the opposite way and the person clings precisely because nothing holds still long enough to trust it.

Some of this is chosen: real facility with transition, purpose found in the movement itself. Some of it isn't—movement as an exit from vulnerability instead of a route through it, kept up not because anywhere is worth staying but because staying is what's avoided. The two look identical from outside. They are not identical from inside.

What's left, after enough years of it, is someone who can tell you where they've been in more detail than anyone should have to sit through, and can't tell you what any of it came to—someone who's been browsing the whole time and never once bought anything.

**Note added 2026-09-06:** the epistemic line now reads COGITA·DISCE·NECTE·FARE—_fare_, the imperative of the deponent _fari_, "to speak", in place of _enuntia_, "declare"—and PERCIPE·STRUE·EFFICE is no longer one of my mottos. The reading below is left as it was written.

> COGITA·DISCE·NECTE·ENUNTIA  
> PERCIPE·STRUE·EFFICE

The two sequences describe a complete epistemic life, divided between an inner and an outer loop.

The first—think, learn, connect, declare—is the movement of mind before it meets the world. It begins alone, turning attention on a problem before anything outside has shaped the answer. Learning follows: the deliberate pursuit of what thinking reveals it cannot yet reach. Connection is the hinge, the moment when discrete materials fuse into something that did not exist in any single source. Declaration closes the loop, turning what the mind synthesized into words others can take up. The sequence is cyclical because declaring produces new questions that restart the thinking. It is a discipline of intellectual honesty: no skipping from thinking straight to assertion, no declaring what has not been thought through.

The second—perceive, connect, effect—operates at a different scale and speed. Where the first is recursive and meditative, the second is transactional and world-facing. Perception takes in a live situation: what is present, what is asked of you, what conditions obtain. Connection is again the hinge, but now it links perception to action rather than learning to language. Effect demands what declaration does not: change. The sequence is compressed because the outer loop must be responsive; dwelling too long in perception or connection is itself a failure.

The two mottos map onto each other as preparation and execution. Connection appears in both: it is the faculty that makes either loop work, the refusal to let inputs remain inert. The inner loop has four steps because formation is slow work, and the outer loop has three because action, once formation is done, should be simple. Speed without depth is merely reaction; depth without action, merely erudition.

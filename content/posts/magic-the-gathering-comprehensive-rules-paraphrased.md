_A casual paraphrase of the Magic: The Gathering Comprehensive Rules, effective November 6, 2026: 313 pages, compressed to one sitting._

## The golden rules (Section 1)

- The card wins. If a card says something that breaks the rules, the card is right, but only for that one situation. The one thing no card can stop is you conceding.
- "Can't" beats "can." If one effect says you may play an extra land and another says you can't play lands, you can't.
- Do what you can. If part of an instruction is impossible, skip that part.
- Whoever's turn it is chooses first. When everyone has to choose at once, the active player picks, then everyone else in turn order, and then it all happens at the same moment. Players call this "APNAP."

## How you win

Everyone starts at 20 life (40 in Commander). You lose if your life hits 0 or less, if you have to draw from an empty library, or if you collect 10 poison counters. In Commander, you also lose if one commander has dealt you 21 combat damage over the game. And you lose if a card says so. A draw is possible.

## Mana

Mana comes in five colors (white, blue, black, red, green) plus colorless, and you mostly get it by tapping lands. You get one land per turn, played in your own main phase when nothing else is going on.

## Card types (Section 3)

Lands make mana. Creatures fight. Artifacts and enchantments sit on the table doing things. Planeswalkers are allies with loyalty points to spend. Battles are things you attack to defeat, usually so you can cast their back face. Instants can be cast almost anytime; sorceries only in your own main phase when nothing else is happening. Anything that stays on the table is a "permanent."

## Where cards live (Section 4)

Library (your deck), hand, battlefield, graveyard, the stack (where spells wait to happen), exile (the "removed from the game" pile), and the command zone (for commanders, emblems, and the like). Ante is still in the rules, as a museum piece.

## A turn (Section 5)

1. Untap everything you control.
2. Upkeep. Your "at the beginning of your upkeep" stuff happens.
3. Draw a card. In a two-player game, the player who goes first skips this on their first turn; in multiplayer games (except Two-Headed Giant), nobody skips it.
4. Main phase. Play your land and cast your stuff.
5. Combat. You declare attackers, they declare blockers, damage happens. Unblocked attackers hit the player, planeswalker, or battle they're attacking.
6. Second main phase. More stuff.
7. End step, then cleanup. Discard down to seven cards, damage wears off everything, and "until end of turn" effects end.

## The stack and priority (Section 6)

This is the heart of the game. When you cast a spell or use an ability, it goes on the stack and waits. Everyone gets a chance to respond, and responses go on top. Once everyone passes in a row, the top item resolves. Last in, first out: whoever responds last goes first. Mana abilities and a few "special actions" skip the stack.

## Four kinds of ability

- Spell: the instructions on an instant or sorcery, followed as it resolves.
- Activated: "Pay this cost: do this thing."
- Triggered: "When," "whenever," or "at": these go off automatically when something happens.
- Static: always on, like "Your creatures get +1/+1."

## Layers and replacement effects

The scary part. When lots of always-on effects pile up, rule 613 sets a strict order for applying them: copy effects first, then control, text, types, colors, abilities, and power/toughness last. "Instead" effects replace an event before it happens; when several could apply, the affected player (or the controller of the affected thing) picks which goes first. Most players never think about any of this until a judge has to.

## State-based actions

The game constantly cleans up after itself. A creature with toughness 0 or less dies. Lethal damage kills. A planeswalker at 0 loyalty is gone. A player at 0 life loses. And if you control two legendary permanents with the same name, you keep one.

## The keyword zoo (Section 7)

Flying, trample, deathtouch, lifelink, and about 190 other keyword abilities, plus some 70 keyword actions like scry, mill, and surveil. Recent additions:

- Blight N: put N -1/-1 counters on one of your own creatures.
- Heal: remove damage from a permanent.
- Recruit: draw, discard, and if what you discarded wasn't a land, make a 1/1 white Human Soldier.
- Assimilate: take a permanent and make it a Borg (yes, really).
- Empower Jace: make or grow a Jace planeswalker token.
- Sneak: during your declare blockers step, return one of your unblocked attackers to hand and pay the sneak cost instead of the mana cost; the new creature comes in tapped and attacking whatever the returned one was.
- Beam me up, Teamwork, and Storied are also new.

## Weird cards

Split, flip, double-faced, meld, saga, adventure, class, case, prototype, omen, and station cards each get their own rules. The newest are **preparation cards**: a permanent with a little spell inset in its text box. When the permanent becomes "prepared," a copy of that inset spell appears in exile and you can cast it. Casting it uses up the "prepared" status.

## Odds and ends

The rules also cover controlling another player's turn (rule 724), the monarch, the initiative, day/night, rad counters, subgames, and restarting the game (rule 728). A whole rule (733) covers shortcuts and loops: you can skip the tedious back-and-forth ("go to combat?") as long as everyone understands what you mean.

## Multiplayer and casual variants (Sections 8 and 9)

Free-for-All, Two-Headed Giant (teams share a life total of 30), Emperor, Grand Melee, Planechase, Archenemy, Conspiracy Draft, and **Commander**: a 100-card singleton deck led by a legendary commander that waits in the command zone. Commander is the format most people play.

## In one sentence

Play a land, cast things, attack, let everyone respond, the last thing in resolves first, the card beats the rulebook, "can't" beats "can," and when in doubt, call a judge.

---
title: Freedom as rejection versus freedom as commitment
description: Freedom involves more than rejecting societal norms; it also requires embracing structures, disciplines, and commitments that enable flourishing, balancing negative liberty with positive liberty.
date: 2026-10-08
---

Freedom, on one view, is defensive: a space carved out against collective constraint, asserted by rejecting the norms of one's society.

This view has obvious appeal. Individual conscience has often defied prevailing norms: abolitionists, suffragists, and civil rights activists all claimed freedom by standing against their society's accepted practices. Their rejections made room for broader liberties. The capacity to say "no" to convention looks foundational to freedom.

But the view is incomplete. Freedom is also the capacity to pursue commitments, relationships, and projects. A person who rejects every norm to stay autonomous ends up cut off from the communities and structures that make richer freedoms possible: collaborative creation, sustained intimacy, a share in common work.

Many liberties require social coordination to exist at all. Property rights, freedom of contract, and protection from violence depend on shared norms and institutions. Rejecting all structure shrinks freedom to a primitive, anxious state where each person must defend every claim alone.

Freedom has two modes. Negative liberty, freedom from interference, involves rejecting illegitimate constraints. Positive liberty, the freedom to achieve, create, and flourish, often requires embracing norms, disciplines, and obligations. The artist develops skill through rigorous practice. The athlete submits to training regimens. The scholar accepts intellectual standards. Each finds freedom through structured commitment.

The strongest freedom does both: it rejects norms that constrain flourishing and keeps the structures that enable it. Reflexive rejection of all norms imprisons as surely as reflexive acceptance; it cuts one off from what communities and traditions provide.

Being free means knowing which walls trap us and which hold up the roof we grow under.

---
title: The arithmetic of forgetting
description: A Poe-style narrator builds an ebony wheel apparatus to statistically measure the decay of memories after his beloved Lenore's death, only to realize quantifying loss cannot preserve or replace lived remembrance.
date: 2026-12-14
---

In the dreary chambers of my ancestral home, I had devoted myself to a singular and most peculiar obsession: the measurement of Memory's decay. For it was my conviction—nay, my very monomania—that each turn of Time's merciless wheel must obliterate the impressions left by its previous revolution. The notion had seized me during that interminable period of solitude following the demise of my beloved Lenore, whose countenance grew daily more spectral in my recollection, features blurring like text on parchment exposed to rain.

My apparatus was of my own devising: a great wheel of polished ebony, its circumference inscribed with silver notations marking the hours, days, and years. Upon its surface I had affixed daguerreotypes—images of Lenore, of my childhood home, of events both momentous and trivial—and I observed with methodical precision how each image faded with every passage beneath the needle I had suspended above the wheel's edge.

I maintained exhaustive ledgers documenting the rate of dissolution, calculating with fevered intensity the mean duration of each memory type, the median persistence of joy versus sorrow, the statistical distribution of recollections across the spectrum of emotional intensity. The mathematics of remembrance became my religion; its formulae, my litany.

"Do you not see?" I cried to my sole companion, a raven that had taken residence upon the bust of Pallas above my chamber door. "The probable error of memory's persistence follows an inexorable pattern! We are condemned not merely to forget, but to forget that we have forgotten!"

The bird regarded me with obsidian eyes that reflected nothing.

It was during a night of particular disquietude, as a tempest raged beyond my shuttered windows, that I made my most terrible discovery. In reviewing my calculations by guttering candlelight, I perceived a ghastly truth: the memories I most desperately clutched—those of Lenore's smile, her voice, the particular way she said my name—were deteriorating at precisely the rate my equations had predicted. Neither love nor agony had preserved them from Time's implacable arithmetic.

In my despair, I flung my inkwell across the room, staining the wall with a black blot that seemed to mock my efforts. The raven stirred—was it "Nevermore"? No, it uttered nothing, yet in its silence lay an accusation: that I had squandered what remained of Lenore in my obsessive measurement of her disappearance.

For what is measurement but a spectator's pastime? To calculate the rate of memory's decay is still to watch it fade rather than to live within its rapidly dissolving borders. In my meticulous documentation of loss, I had become a ghoulish accountant, tabulating absence.

I approached the ebony wheel, my fingers trembling as they traced its circumference. The daguerreotypes had faded—some to near invisibility—but in that moment of lucidity, I understood that the wheel was a toy beside the thing itself. We are the wheel. My ledgers recorded only the losses I had noticed, a few drops from an ocean of oblivion.

As dawn's gray light filtered through my window, I made a final notation in my journal: "The statistical distribution of memory records only the pattern of our forgetting. We reach backward in reconstruction, forward in anticipation, while the present moment, the only reality we might truly possess, turns beneath the needle and is gone."

The raven took flight, startled perhaps by my sudden movement or by the wild laughter that escaped my lips as I closed the ledger for the final time. For I had at last understood the supreme jest of my endeavor: that in my quest to quantify memory's dissolution, I had forgotten how to remember.

---
title: Why you cannot notice the last time
description: Lastness is a relation assigned retrospectively by memory, not a perceptible quality of a moment, so the advice to "notice the last time" demands an impossible perception and produces unearned guilt. Finitude, unlike lastness, is present all along.
date: 2026-09-11
---

The advice is meant kindly. There will be a last time you carry a child to bed, a last ordinary conversation with a friend, a last unremarkable Sunday in the house you grew up in. So pay attention, because no one will tell you which one it is. Notice more. Hold it while you have it.

It cannot be done, and not because we are careless. The thing we are told to notice is not there to be noticed.

Lastness is not a property of a moment. Nothing distinguishes the final conversation from the second-to-last one; no quality inheres in it that closer attention would have caught. What makes it the last is everything that failed to follow—a relation between that afternoon and a future that never arrived. The relation is real enough once it exists. It does not exist yet when we are told to perceive it. Memory assigns lastness afterward, from a vantage that knows what came next; the assigning is when the property begins.

So the injunction asks attention to point at something not in the room. This is not a difficult perception. It is not a perception. You cannot attend harder to a fact that will not be a fact for another eleven years.

The damage is that the failure feels like ours anyway. The advice arrives as wisdom, cannot be followed, and then hands us the bill. We didn't notice, so we must have been distracted, ungrateful, asleep at the moment that turned out to matter. It is a cruel structure: it can be applied only backward, only after the moment is unreachable, and only by a self who now knows something the earlier self could not have known. We are convicted of failing to read a signal that was never broadcast.

What is true in the advice survives the correction. Attention does change experience, and it works on what is present. Finitude is present. That the sequence ends is a standing fact of every afternoon in it, available at all times, requiring no forecast and no guess at which one. Which afternoon is the last is available at no time. Confusing them turns a livable awareness into an impossible vigilance, and then into guilt.

You did not miss it. There was nothing to miss: only an afternoon indistinguishable from the others, doing what afternoons do. Lastness is a verdict memory passes, not a signal the world sends, and no amount of noticing would have brought the verdict in any sooner.

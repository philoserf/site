---
title: Hell Yes or No Heuristic
description: Derek Sivers' heuristic for eliminating lukewarm commitments by only accepting opportunities that generate immediate enthusiasm.
date: 2025-12-29
---

A decision-making heuristic popularized by [Derek Sivers](https://sive.rs/hellyeah): tepid enthusiasm indicates misalignment, not compromise.

The framework eliminates middle ground: no "maybe" or "I should." When an opportunity draws a qualified response ("sounds interesting, but…" or "I guess I could…"), the hesitation is a preference you haven't admitted to yourself.

A friend invites you to a networking event. Your response: "I should probably go—it could be useful." That qualified answer reveals the truth: you don't want to attend.

Genuine alignment produces clarity. Hesitation indicates competing values, hidden costs, or social pressure overriding actual desire. The "hell yes" response skips the analysis and asks your gut. Your immediate emotional reaction contains information that rational deliberation obscures or rationalizes away.

Most applicable to discretionary choices where you have genuine agency—social invitations, creative projects, career opportunities, relationship decisions. Less applicable to necessary obligations or situations where immediate enthusiasm isn't the relevant metric. Long-term skill development requires commitment through stretches when you don't feel like it.

Attention and time are scarce. In environments of artificial abundance—infinite entertainment, social obligations, professional opportunities—saying yes by default scatters your effort and breeds resentment. Raising the threshold leaves room for commitments that sustain your energy rather than drain it.

The heuristic can excuse you from the discomfort of growing. Expanding capacity often requires accepting opportunities that provoke anxiety rather than enthusiasm. It works best once you've established baseline competence and are refining what you already do rather than learning to do it.

"Hell yes or no" is a trigger phrase: it interrupts the automatic yes and makes you check whether you're acting from authentic preference or social conditioning.

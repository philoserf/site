## Curated Reading Paths

### Military Science Fiction and Space Warfare

- _The Face of Battle_ by John Keegan (1976): what combat is like for the soldier
- _Supplying War_ by Martin van Creveld (1977): how armies are actually supplied
- _Dorsai!_ by Gordon R. Dickson (1959): where the genre's warrior cultures begin
- _Hospital Station_ (Sector General) by James White (1962): a break, with medicine in place of war
- _Necromancer_ (Childe Cycle) by Gordon R. Dickson (1962): Dickson at his most philosophical
- _Retief: Envoy to New Worlds_ by Keith Laumer (1963): diplomats and bureaucrats, played for laughs
- _The Forever War_ by Joe Haldeman (1974): Vietnam as interstellar war
- _The Mercenary_ by Jerry Pournelle (1977): soldiering as a business
- _Hammer's Slammers_ by David Drake (1979): mercenary tank warfare, told from the ground
- _Sky Raiders_ by William H. Keith Jr. (1982): space marines
- _Revelation Space_ by Alastair Reynolds (2000): war under real physics
- _Decider_ by L. E. Modesitt Jr. (2001)
- _The Praxis_ by Walter Jon Williams (2002): fleet strategy at scale

### Medieval Social History

- _The Time Traveler's Guide to Medieval England_ by Ian Mortimer (2008): what daily life was like
- _Life in a Medieval Village_ by Frances and Joseph Gies (1990): how a village was organized
- _The Return of Martin Guerre_ by Natalie Zemon Davis (1983): a microhistory that reads like a detective story
- _Montaillou_ by Emmanuel Le Roy Ladurie (1975): one village's beliefs, rebuilt from Inquisition records
- _The Good Men_ by Charmaine Craig (2002): a novel of the Cathars

### Celtic Britain and Arthurian Legend

- _The Mabinogion_ translated by Sioned Davies (2007): the Welsh myths themselves
- _Asterix in Britain_ by René Goscinny and Albert Uderzo (1966): Romans meet Britons, played for laughs
- _The Eagle of the Ninth_ by Rosemary Sutcliff (1954): Roman Britain, and the classic place to start
- _The Lantern Bearers_ by Rosemary Sutcliff (1959): the Romans leave
- _The Crystal Cave_ by Mary Stewart (1970): Merlin's youth, leading into Arthur
- _The Winter King_ by Bernard Cornwell (1995): Arthur without the magic
- _Dreaming the Eagle_ by Manda Scott (2003): Boudica's rebellion from the Celtic side
- _Isolde_ by Joan Allen (1989): Tristan and Isolde as romance
- _The High Kings_ by Joy Chant (1983–1988): the British legends retold as one epic
- _Down the Long Wind_ by Gillian Bradshaw (1980–1982): Gawain's story, to finish
